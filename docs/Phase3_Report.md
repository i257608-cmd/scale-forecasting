## Summary

- **Task:** we forecast **how soon a production choke's restriction will grow by 5 %**, from 1-minute
  sensor data. The output is a calibrated probability for six horizons (1 h to 72 h) plus an
  uncertainty interval on the time-to-event.
- **Evaluation:** strictly on **wells never seen in training**, using leave-one-well-out over the 6 wells
  with real scaling. In total that is 24 real events, 248 negative well-days and 1,119 real instances.
- **PI-PatchSurv vs the strongest baseline (gradient-boosted trees, B2):**
  - **Threshold-free ranking:** about equal. AUPRC@24h is 0.667 vs 0.679 (difference not
    significant, p = 0.89). PI-PatchSurv has the best mean rank over folds (2.8 vs 3.2), the best
    C-index (0.85), the best Brier score (0.13) and the best calibration (ECE 0.17 vs 0.23).
  - **Alarms with a threshold fixed before seeing the test well:** PI-PatchSurv is clearly better. It
    catches **17 of 24 events (71 %) with a median lead of 3.6 h**, while B2 catches at most 6 of 24.
    It raises **zero false alarms on 594 normal instances and on all class-6 "quick restriction"
    look-alikes**. All of its false alarms come from *hydrate in production line* (class 8) and
    class 9, so the overall rate is 0.23 per well-day, still above the 1-per-well-month target.
- **Every other deep sequence baseline is beaten** (LSTM, GRU, CNN-LSTM, TCN; AUPRC 0.31–0.54).
- **Robustness:** PI-PatchSurv loses only 8 % AUPRC when 3 of 9 sensors drop out; the LSTM loses 48 %.
- **Honest negatives:**
  - Slow-scaling well 22 is not forecast by any learned model (AUPRC ≤ 0.43).
  - Uncertainty intervals reach nominal coverage (89 % for a 90 % target) but are wide.
  - Some single-seed ablation differences are within seed noise.

## 1. Implementation and changes from Phase 2 and class feedback

**Class feedback** on the 3-minute presentation was positive ("that was good"); no change was
requested. The changes below were forced by the data (EDA, `notebooks/01_EDA.ipynb`) and were
approved by the group on 14 Nov 2026.

| Phase 2 plan | Phase 3 implementation | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Forecast the *start* of scaling | Forecast *severity*: time until the restriction proxy RI* reaches +5 % | Pre-onset data has a median of 6.5 h only, so forecasting the start offers at most a few hours. Scaling is gradual, so a severity target is meaningful (hours on well 24, days on well 22). |
| RI = ΔP_PCK / ΔP_ref(opening) | RI* = P-MON-CKP / own pre-onset median (label); own first-hour median (causal feature) | P-JUS-CKP and T-MON-CKP are missing in 100 % of real class-7 instances and choke opening in 78 %, so ΔP cannot be computed. |
| ΔT across the choke | T-JUS-CKP alone (Joule–Thomson cooling signal) | T-MON-CKP is missing. |
| Class 7 label = 7 | Onset = first label 107 *or* 7 | 31 of 36 real instances only carry the transient label 107. |
| SI(T, P) chemistry prior (PHREEQC) | Not built | Time. Listed as a limitation (gap G2 only partly addressed). |
| B7 Transformer-LSTM, B9 LSTM-AE | Not run | Time. The patch Transformer without physics (E2 "−physics") is the closest attention baseline. |
| LightGBM | scikit-learn HistGradientBoosting | Same algorithm family; LightGBM needs a system library missing on macOS. |

**Bugs found and fixed during implementation:**
- **Hand-drawn instances store pressures in bar, real instances in Pa.** Unconverted, the LSTM learned
  "low pressure = scaling" and ranked well 24 inverted (AUROC 0.10). Fixed by converting ×10⁵.
- **Sentinel readings of 1.18 × 10⁴² Pa (well 6) overflowed float32.** Values with |x| ≥ 10⁹ are now
  treated as missing.
- **The calibration-well threshold did not transfer.** It was 0.9999 for B2, giving recall 0. Thresholds
  for B0–B2 are now chosen on out-of-fold scores over the training wells.

## 2. Dataset, preprocessing, features, setup and splits

**Data:**
- Petrobras 3W v2.0.0, pinned to commit 826fec4, CC BY 4.0.
- 2,228 instances at 1 Hz: 1,119 real from 40 wells, 1,089 simulated, 20 hand-drawn.
- The target class 7, *Scaling in PCK*, has 36 real instances from 6 wells (24: 19, 22: 8, 23: 4,
  21: 2, 6: 2, 1: 1) plus 10 hand-drawn; there are no simulated class-7 instances.
- 24 of the 36 real instances reach the +5 % event (well 24: 17, 22: 3, 6: 2, 1: 1, 21: 1, 23: 0).

**Preprocessing** (`src/data/preprocess.py`):
- Resample 1 Hz to 1-minute means.
- Sensors frozen for more than 30 min, or missing, become **mask channels**.
- Gaps of 10 min or less are forward-filled.
- Variables missing in more than 50 % of real instances are dropped. That leaves 9 channels:
  P-MON-CKP, T-JUS-CKP, P-TPT, T-TPT, P-ANULAR, P-PDG, P-JUS-CKGL, T-PDG and ESTADO-SDV-P.
- Hand-drawn pressures are converted from bar to Pa; |x| ≥ 10⁹ is treated as missing.
- Robust scaling (median / IQR) is fitted per fold on training files only.

**Labels:**
- Onset t₀ is the first minute labelled 107 or 7.
- The event time t_E is the first time the 60-min rolling median of RI* reaches 1.05 or more after t₀.
- Windows every 10 min before t_E get τ = t_E − t, binned at 1, 3, 6, 12, 24 and 72 h (7 bins).
- Windows after t_E are dropped.
- Normal instances and non-crossing scaling instances are right-censored.
- RI* crossings in other classes (hydrate, slugging) are *not* events.
- This gives 109,547 windows: 44,386 real, 62,058 simulated and 3,103 hand-drawn.

**Features** (`src/features/build.py`):
- The last 360 minutes of the 9 channels, their masks, and RI*. RI* uses a causal baseline: the
  median of the instance's first hour, which always comes before onset.
- 36 physics features: rolling mean, standard deviation and slope over 10, 60 and 360 min of RI*,
  P-MON-CKP, T-JUS-CKP and P-TPT.

**Splits** (`src/data/splits.py`): 6 leave-one-well-out folds, one per scaling well.
- **Test:** all real instances of that well, plus one round-robin group of non-scaling wells. Every
  real instance is tested exactly once.
- **Calibration:** the next scaling well that has an event (never well 24, which holds 17 of the 24
  events), plus another group of non-scaling wells.
- **Training:** all remaining real wells plus all simulated and hand-drawn instances. Synthetic data
  never enters calibration or test.
- **Threshold:** never chosen on test wells.

**Model — PI-PatchSurv** (`src/models/seq.py`, 128,775 parameters):
1. **Sequence branch:** channel-independent patch Transformer (patch 16, stride 8, 44 tokens per
   channel). Each mask is folded into its value's patch token. 2 layers, d = 64, 4 heads, giving z_s.
2. **Physics branch:** an MLP (2 × 64, GELU, dropout 0.1) on the 36 physics features, giving z_p.
3. **Gated fusion:** g = σ(W[z_s; z_p]), z = g ⊙ z_s + (1 − g) ⊙ z_p. The mean gate is 0.48 (SD 0.04
   over 30 runs), so the model relies on both branches about equally.
4. **Discrete-time survival head:** h_k = σ(w_kᵀz + b_k), F(k) = 1 − Π(1 − h_j). F is monotone in the
   horizon by construction.

**Training:**
- **Loss:** censor-aware survival NLL, class-weighted, plus 0.1 · L_mono, which penalises drops in F
  between consecutive windows of a class-7 instance.
- **Optimiser:** AdamW, lr 10⁻³, batch 256, 12 epochs × 150 steps.
- **Early stopping:** on calibration-well loss.
- **Ensemble:** 5 seeds, averaged.

**Uncertainty:**
- Deep ensemble.
- Split-conformal interval on log time-to-event, calibrated on the calibration well's event windows,
  90 % target.

**Baselines:**
- B0: threshold on the 60-min mean of RI*.
- B1: logistic regression.
- B2: gradient-boosted trees.
- B3 LSTM, B4 GRU, B5 CNN-LSTM, B6 TCN: same inputs, per-horizon BCE heads, same training budget and
  same early stopping. B0–B2 use the 63 tabular features at the window end.

**Hardware:** MacBook Air M4 (PyTorch MPS, float32). PI-PatchSurv takes about 8–10 min per fold and
seed. Total compute was about 10 h.

| **This work** | **Forecasting severity (+5 %) of class 7** | **3W 2.0, leave-one-well-out, unseen wells, real-only test** | AUPRC@24h 0.67, C-index 0.85; deployed recall 0.71 | **median 3.6 h** (IQR 4.8; up to days on the 72-h head) | **ensemble + conformal, ECE 0.17** |

**Why the numbers cannot be compared directly (fairness limitations):**
1. The published 3W studies *detect* an anomaly that is already labelled. We *forecast* a future
   severity level before it happens. A detector is scored after onset, our model before t_E.
2. They split randomly or at sample level. Windows of the same well and instance then sit in both
   training and test, which inflates scores. We hold out whole wells; on 3W this is the harder and
   more realistic setting (gap G3).
3. They report accuracy or F1 on balanced or sample-level sets. Our positives are about 14 % of the
   labelled 24-h windows and we report AUPRC, event recall and false alarms per day; accuracy would be
   above 85 % for a constant "no event" model.
4. Lai 2026 forecasts water breakthrough in a different field. Su 2024 uses chemistry, not sensors.
   Only the task framing (time-to-event, cross-well) is comparable.
5. We did not re-run the published models. B3 (LSTM) and B5 (CNN-LSTM) follow the architectures common
   in that literature and serve as their proxies under our protocol.

No published study we found forecasts 3W class 7 with a lead time. This supports gap G1.

## 6. Ablation studies (E2)

Each ablation is **one seed**, so it is compared with **PI-PatchSurv seed 0** (same seed), not with
the 5-seed ensemble. Δ = reference − ablation on pooled AUPRC@24h, from a paired instance bootstrap
with Holm correction across all 12 comparisons.

| Variant | AUPRC@24h (mean of folds) | AUROC@24h | Recall @ 1 FA/month | Deployed recall / FA per day (best strategy) | Δ pooled AUPRC [95 % CI], Holm p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PI-PatchSurv, 5-seed ensemble | 0.667 | 0.900 | 0.50 | 0.71 / 0.234 | +0.057 [0.013, 0.133] vs seed 0, p < 0.001 |
| **PI-PatchSurv, seed 0 (reference)** | 0.598 | 0.820 | 0.50 | 0.67 / 0.311 | — |
| − physics branch | 0.585 | 0.723 | 0.38 | 0.38 / 0.028 | −0.048 [−0.18, 0.06], p = 1.0 |
| − L_mono | 0.574 | 0.796 | 0.50 | 0.71 / 0.270 | −0.016 [−0.04, −0.003], p = 0.07 |
| − survival head (per-horizon BCE) | 0.644 | 0.885 | 0.63 | 0.33 / 0.065 | +0.035 [−0.04, 0.20], p = 1.0 |
| patch 8, no overlap (vs 16/8) | 0.586 | 0.844 | 0.63 | 0.08 / 0.089 | −0.243 [−0.37, −0.13], p < 0.001 |

**What the ablations show:**
- **Ensemble (clearest effect).** It improves AUPRC by 0.057 (significant), Brier from 0.185 to 0.133
  and ECE from 0.213 to 0.165. Single seeds vary a lot: AUPRC on well 21 ranges 0.18–0.95 over 5
  seeds. A deep ensemble is necessary, not optional, with 24 events.
- **Physics branch.** Removing it costs 0.10 AUROC and 0.12 recall at the budget. The C-index drops
  from 0.80 to 0.68 and ECE worsens from 0.21 to 0.31. The pooled-AUPRC difference is not significant.
- **L_mono.** Small; removing it does not hurt pooled AUPRC (Δ −0.016, i.e. slightly better without it).
- **Survival head vs per-horizon heads.** About equal on ranking. The survival head gives
  monotone horizon curves and better 3–6 h AUPRC (0.24 / 0.31 vs 0.09 / 0.26). The BCE heads are
  better at 72 h.
- **Patch 8 without overlap.** Higher *pooled* AUPRC (its scores are more comparable across wells) but
  lower mean per-fold AUPRC, and it collapses under the deployed threshold.
- **Not tested: "−synthetic data".** Drawn instances supply the only extra class-7 positives, and
  removing them needs a separate run.

## 7. Results analysis and discussion vs gaps G1–G4

**G1 — no timed forecasting of scaling.**
- PI-PatchSurv gives a calibrated probability for six horizons, catching 71 % of events on unseen
  wells with a median lead of 3.6 h.
- The horizon analysis (`horizon_auprc.png`) shows AUPRC rising from 0.05 at 1 h to 0.67 at 24 h and
  0.70 at 72 h. The longer horizons are easier because most pre-event windows are within a few days.
- Lead time is bounded by the data: pre-onset normal data has a median of 6.5 h, and the time from
  window start to event has a median of 12 h. Multi-day lead is reached only on slow wells, where the
  model fails (well 22). Achieving the literature's 6-day lead for scaling needs longer real histories
  than 3W provides.

**G2 — chemistry and sensors never fused.**
- *Partly addressed.* The physics branch fuses engineered physical indicators (RI* trend,
  Joule–Thomson cooling, system pressure) with learned sequence features through a gate.
- The gate is about 0.48, so both branches are used. Removing physics costs 0.10 AUROC.
- The PHREEQC saturation-index prior was not built: 3W has no water chemistry, and the USGS-based
  prior would be a field-average constant. This remains future work.

**G3 — no unseen-well evaluation.** Fully addressed:
- Every number here is leave-one-well-out with real-only test sets and thresholds fixed before
  testing.
- The central finding is that **ranking transfers across wells but raw score thresholds do not.**
  B2 has 0.63 recall threshold-free but 0.25 deployed.
- Self-referencing the score to the instance's own first hour, the same idea as RI*, more than
  doubles PI-PatchSurv's deployed recall (9 → 17 hits) and cuts false alarms by 60 %.

**G4 — rare events, private data, no uncertainty.**
- The data is public 3W.
- Rarity is handled with a censor-aware loss, class weighting and synthetic data in training only.
- Uncertainty comes from the deep ensemble plus conformal intervals. The 90 % conformal interval
  covers 89 % of test event windows for PI-PatchSurv, while B2 covers only 72 %: its residuals
  calibrated on one well do not transfer. The PI-PatchSurv interval is wide, though, spanning a
  factor of about 9 either side of the predicted time.
- The ensemble gives the best Brier score (0.13) and ECE (0.17) of all models.

**Robustness (E3, PI-PatchSurv ensemble vs LSTM, corruptions applied to test inputs):**

| Corruption | PI-PatchSurv AUPRC@24h | LSTM (B3) AUPRC@24h |
|---|---|---|
| clean | 0.667 | 0.536 |
| Gaussian noise 1 / 3 / 5 % | 0.671 / 0.706 / 0.751 | 0.534 / 0.534 / 0.532 |
| 1 / 2 / 3 sensors dropped (value → median, mask = 1) | 0.630 / 0.628 / 0.616 (−8 %) | 0.415 / 0.342 / 0.280 (−48 %) |
| one sensor frozen for 60 min, undetected | 0.667 | 0.527 |

- **Dropout:** the mask-aware, channel-independent design degrades gracefully.
- **Noise:** the AUPRC *increase* under noise is unexpected. A likely cause is that noise blurs
  fine-scale patterns the model over-fits on training wells, which acts as test-time regularisation.
  It should not be read as a benefit.
- **Limitation:** the physics features were left clean, and the resampling test (30 s–5 min) was not
  run.

**Statistical significance:**
- A Friedman test over the 5 folds with events is not significant (χ² = 11.1, p = 0.13).
- PI-PatchSurv has the best mean rank (2.8), ahead of B2 (3.2) and B3 (3.4).
- Paired instance bootstrap with Holm correction: PI-PatchSurv beats B1 significantly. Against B5
  (p_raw = 0.02) and B4 it is better but not significant after correction. Against B2 the difference
  is 0.018 (p = 0.89).
- With 24 events concentrated in one well (17 in well 24), this study cannot separate the top models
  statistically. We state this plainly rather than claim superiority.

**Why does a GBDT match a Transformer here?**
- The informative signal is a slow trend in 2–3 channels, which the hand-made rolling slopes capture
  directly.
- Only 24 events exist, too few for a deep model to beat good features.
- PI-PatchSurv's advantage is not raw ranking. It is (i) calibrated, monotone multi-horizon
  probabilities, (ii) far fewer false alarms on normal operation (0 vs 50 episodes), (iii) robustness
  to sensor loss, and (iv) deployed recall on unseen wells.

**Training-dynamics finding:** calibration-well loss was lowest after the first epoch in 28 of 30
PI-PatchSurv runs (and for all sequence baselines). Models over-fit the training wells within about
150 steps. Choosing the epoch by calibration AUPRC instead was tried; it gave no signal (flat at
about 0.05, because one calibration well has only 1–3 events), so the loss rule was kept for all
models. Better cross-well regularisation, for example per-well normalisation or domain-adversarial
training, is the most promising next step.

**Limitations:**
1. 24 real events, 17 of them from one well. Results are dominated by well 24.
2. RI* does not control for choke-opening changes; the opening sensor is missing in 78 %.
3. Slow-scaling wells 22 and 23 are not forecast.
4. The false-alarm budget of 1 per well-month is not met; hydrate events (class 8) are the main
   confounder.
5. Ablations use a single seed.
6. Not done: B7, B9, the SI prior, Optuna tuning (all models used the same fixed budget), the
   resampling test, and the v1-wells → v2-wells generalisation test.
7. The class-7 numbers for published work come from the authors' theses because the publisher pages
   could not be accessed.

**Future work:**
- Per-well domain adaptation to fix threshold transfer.
- Hydrate-vs-scale discrimination using T-JUS-CKP dynamics.
- Longer real histories for multi-day lead.
- A PHREEQC saturation index once produced-water chemistry per well is available.

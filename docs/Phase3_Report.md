# Phase 3 — Research Implementation and Experimental Evaluation

**Forecasting time to choke restriction from mineral scale with production sensors:
a physics-informed survival model and a strict unseen-well benchmark on Petrobras 3W v2.0.0**

## Summary

- **Task.** From 1-minute production sensors, forecast **how soon the restriction of a production
  choke will grow by 5 %** (3W class 7, *Scaling in PCK*). The output is a probability for six horizons
  (1 h to 72 h) and an interval on the time-to-event. No published 3W work forecasts class 7 with a
  lead time (§5).
- **Evaluation.** Leave-one-well-out over the 6 wells with real scaling: 24 real events, about 248
  negative well-days, all 1,119 real instances tested once. Thresholds are fixed on calibration wells
  before a test well is seen. 12 models (threshold rule, 2 classical, 8 deep, our PI-PatchSurv), each
  with fixed and Optuna-tuned hyper-parameters on an equal budget.
- **Best deployed alarms:** the tuned **GRU (B4)** catches **17 of 24 events at 0.052 false alarms per
  well-day (about 1.6 per well-month), median lead 2.9 h**; the tuned **Transformer-LSTM (B7)** catches
  16 of 24 at 0.044 per well-day, median lead 4.1 h. The target of at most 1 false alarm per well-month
  is close but not met.
- **Best ranking:** the LSTM (B3) has the best per-fold AUPRC@24h (0.81, AUROC 0.95); PatchTST (B8) and
  the per-horizon variant of our model have the best pooled AUPRC (0.75 and 0.82).
- **PI-PatchSurv (our proposed model) is not the best model.** Its 5-seed ensemble reaches AUPRC@24h
  0.70 (pooled 0.54) and catches 9 of 24 events deployed. Its real strengths are elsewhere: it keeps its
  score when 3 of 9 sensors fail (0.70 → 0.70, LSTM 0.81 → 0.56), its conformal intervals reach close to
  nominal coverage (87 % for a 90 % target, most deep baselines 40–60 %), and removing its physics branch
  costs 0.07 AUROC.
- **Statistics:** only "the deep models beat the threshold rule, logistic regression and the
  autoencoder detector" survives Holm correction. The Friedman test over 12 models is borderline
  (p = 0.06) and no pair of deep models differs significantly ({{NEMENYI}}). With 24 events, 17 from one
  well, this study cannot rank the deep models; we say so instead of claiming a winner.
- **A data bug changed the conclusions.** 885 of 2,228 instance file names repeat across class folders
  in 3W; keying arrays by file name fed about half of the synthetic training windows another class's
  signal. After the fix (16 Nov) every sequence model was re-run: plain LSTM/GRU jumped from AUPRC
  0.3–0.5 to 0.7–0.8 and overtook PI-PatchSurv. The pre-fix report (15 Nov) is superseded.

## 1. Implementation and changes from Phase 2 and class feedback

**Class feedback** on the 3-minute presentation was positive ("that was good"); no change was requested.
The changes below were forced by the data (EDA, `notebooks/01_EDA.ipynb`) or by compute, and were agreed
in the group.

| Phase 2 plan | Phase 3 implementation | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Forecast the *start* of scaling | Forecast *severity*: time until the restriction proxy RI* reaches +5 % | Pre-onset data has a median of 6.5 h only; scaling is gradual, so a severity target is meaningful (hours on well 24, days on well 22). |
| RI = ΔP_PCK / ΔP_ref(opening) | RI* = P-MON-CKP / own pre-onset median (label); own first-hour median (causal feature) | P-JUS-CKP and T-MON-CKP are missing in 100 % of real class-7 instances and choke opening in 78 %, so ΔP cannot be computed. |
| ΔT across the choke | T-JUS-CKP alone (Joule–Thomson cooling) | T-MON-CKP is missing. |
| Class 7 label = 7 | Onset = first label 107 *or* 7 | 31 of 36 real instances carry only the transient label 107. |
| SI(T, P) prior from USGS PWGD v2.3 + PHREEQC | USGS PWGD **v3.0**, Gulf-of-Mexico offshore brines, PHREEQC (Pitzer) SI for calcite and barite on a T × P grid → `-SI` variants | v2.3 download is blocked by a ScienceBase bot check; v3.0 is served by the USGS ArcGIS service. |
| LightGBM | scikit-learn HistGradientBoosting | Same algorithm family; LightGBM needs a system library missing on macOS. |
| Optuna, equal budget | 20 trials per fold per model, TPE (seed 0), median pruning, objective = calibration-well validation loss; nested per LOWO fold | Calibration AUPRC was flat (one well, 1–3 events), the loss uses every calibration window. |
| "−patching" ablation | Patch length 1 (one token per minute), gradient accumulation over micro-batches of 32 for the same effective batch of 256 | Patch 1 gives 360 tokens per channel and runs out of MPS memory at batch 256. |
| Train on 21 v1 wells → test on 21 new v2 wells | v1.1.1 has **18** real wells; mapped to v2 IDs by time; train on wells 1, 6, 21 (4 events), test on wells 22–24 (20 events), calibrate on well 6 | Correction of a Phase-2 counting error. |
| All runs on the M4 | B1–B4 tuned on the M4 (MPS); B5–B9 and PI-PatchSurv tuned on Kaggle (2 × T4); all final training on the M4 | Local tuning took up to 3.5 h per fold for one model. |

**Bugs found and fixed during implementation (honest record):**
1. **File-name collision across class folders (major).** Synthetic file names such as `DRAWN_00001`
   exist in several class folders (885 of 2,228 instances). `FoldData` keyed arrays by file name, so
   about half of the synthetic training windows, including the 10 hand-drawn scaling drawings, received
   another class's signal. Fixed by keying `label/file` (`trainer.ikey`, regression test in
   `tests/`). Real files are unique, so test and calibration windows and the tabular models B0–B2 were
   never affected; every sequence model was re-run.
2. **Hand-drawn pressures are in bar, real ones in Pa.** Unconverted, the LSTM learned "low pressure =
   scaling" (AUROC 0.10 on well 24). Converted ×10⁵.
3. **Sentinel readings of 1.18 × 10⁴² Pa (well 6)** overflowed float32; |x| ≥ 10⁹ is treated as missing.
4. **Raw calibration-well thresholds did not transfer** (B2 threshold 0.9999, recall 0). Thresholds are
   now also computed on a self-referenced score ("calib-self", §3) and, for B0–B2, out-of-fold on the
   training wells.

Items 1 and 2 are data issues of 3W itself and are not, as far as we could find, reported in the 3W
issue tracker; we will report them to the maintainers.

## 2. Dataset, preprocessing, features, setup and splits

**Data:** Petrobras 3W v2.0.0 (commit 826fec4, CC BY 4.0): 2,228 instances at 1 Hz, 1,119 real from 40
wells, 1,089 simulated, 20 hand-drawn. Class 7 has 36 real instances from 6 wells (24: 19, 22: 8,
23: 4, 21: 2, 6: 2, 1: 1) and 10 hand-drawn; no simulated ones. 24 of the 36 real instances reach the
+5 % event (well 24: 17, 22: 3, 6: 2, 1: 1, 21: 1, 23: 0).

**Preprocessing** (`src/data/preprocess.py`): 1 Hz → 1-minute means; sensors frozen > 30 min or missing
become **mask channels**; gaps ≤ 10 min forward-filled; variables missing in > 50 % of real instances
dropped, leaving 9 channels (P-MON-CKP, T-JUS-CKP, P-TPT, T-TPT, P-ANULAR, P-PDG, P-JUS-CKGL, T-PDG,
ESTADO-SDV-P); hand-drawn pressures bar → Pa; |x| ≥ 10⁹ → missing; robust scaling (median / IQR) fitted
per fold on training files only.

**Labels:** onset t₀ = first minute labelled 107 or 7. Event time t_E = first time the 60-min rolling
median of RI* reaches ≥ 1.05 after t₀. Windows every 10 min before t_E get τ = t_E − t, binned at 1, 3,
6, 12, 24, 72 h (7 bins); windows after t_E are dropped; normal and non-crossing instances are
right-censored; RI* crossings in other classes are not events. 109,547 windows (44,386 real).

**Features** (`src/features/build.py`): the last 360 minutes of the 9 channels, their masks and RI*
(causal baseline = median of the instance's first hour). 36 physics features: rolling mean, SD and slope
over 10, 60, 360 min of RI*, P-MON-CKP, T-JUS-CKP and P-TPT. `-SI` variants add the PHREEQC saturation
index of calcite and barite at the window's T and P (`src/features/si.py`).

**Splits** (`src/data/splits.py`), 6 leave-one-well-out folds, one per scaling well:
- **Test:** all real instances of that well plus one round-robin group of non-scaling wells; every real
  instance is tested exactly once.
- **Calibration:** the next scaling well with an event (never well 24) plus another non-scaling group.
- **Training:** all other real wells plus all simulated and hand-drawn instances (synthetic data never
  enters calibration or test).
- **v1 → v2 split** (E4): train on wells known in 3W v1.1.1, test on wells new in v2 (§7).

**PI-PatchSurv** (`src/models/seq.py`, 128,775 parameters):
1. **Sequence branch:** channel-independent patch Transformer (patch 16, stride 8, 44 tokens per
   channel; each mask folded into its value token; 2 layers, d = 64, 4 heads) → z_s.
2. **Physics branch:** MLP (2 × 64, GELU, dropout 0.1) on the 36 physics features → z_p.
3. **Gated fusion:** g = σ(W[z_s; z_p]), z = g ⊙ z_s + (1 − g) ⊙ z_p.
4. **Discrete-time survival head:** h_k = σ(w_kᵀz + b_k), F(k) = 1 − Π_{j≤k}(1 − h_j), monotone in k.

**Training:** censor-aware, class-weighted survival NLL + 0.1 · L_mono (penalises drops of F between
consecutive windows of a class-7 instance); AdamW, lr 10⁻³, batch 256, 12 epochs × 150 steps; early
stopping on calibration-well loss; 5-seed deep ensemble. **Uncertainty:** ensemble + split-conformal
interval on log time-to-event, calibrated on calibration-well event windows (90 % target).

**Baselines:** B0 threshold on the 60-min mean of RI*; B1 logistic regression; B2 gradient-boosted
trees (63 tabular features); B3 LSTM; B4 GRU; B5 CNN-LSTM; B6 TCN; B7 Transformer-LSTM (Lai et al.
2026 style); B8 PatchTST (our patch encoder without physics, BCE heads); B8i iTransformer; B9 LSTM
autoencoder detector (anomaly score, Lopes 2025 / Aranha 2025 style). B3–B8i share inputs, per-horizon
BCE heads, training budget and early stopping with PI-PatchSurv.

**Hardware:** MacBook Air M4 (PyTorch MPS, float32) for all final training; Kaggle 2 × T4 for part of
the tuning. PI-PatchSurv: about 8–10 min per fold and seed; Optuna tuning took up to 3.5 h per fold
for one model locally and about 3 h for five models on Kaggle (`results/tuning/`).

## 5. Comparison with published work

A focused literature check (17 Nov 2026: web search, the 3W GitHub issue tracker, SPE / OnePetro,
arXiv) found no survival, time-to-event or remaining-useful-life model on 3W, and no forecast of class
7 with a lead time.

| Study | Task | Data / split | Metric (class 7 where available) | Lead time | Uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lopes et al. 2025, EAAI 159:111514 | Open-world detection and classification (autoencoder, binary classifiers, clustering) | intelligent-well-completion fault data (described in Lopes 2026) | 81 % global clustering accuracy† | none | not stated |
| Aranha et al. 2024, SPE J 29:1540 | Real-time anomaly detection: LSTM autoencoder + decision diagram | several wells and fields, simulated and real; split not stated | true-positive rate > 90 % in early stages | "early stages", no hours | none |
| Aranha et al. 2025, Pet. Expl. Dev. 52:1029 | Transformer anomaly detection, intelligent well completions | not verified (abstract not accessible) | not verified | none | none |
| Lopes et al. 2026, arXiv 2608.04041 | LLM agent layer on the open-world pipeline | 3W, 989 real segments, not split by well | class 7 novelty detection 94.9 % [90.5, 97.3] (166/175); top-1 38.9 % (n = 36) | none | Wilson CI |
| 3W issue #231 (Oct 2026, under review) | Classification benchmark, held-out wells | 3W 2.0, 10 sealed wells | macro-F1 0.30 held-out vs 0.99 random split | onset ≤ 300 s metric | bootstrap over wells |
| Cheng et al., SPE (CNN-LSTM) | Scale detection and magnitude | field well histories | > 95 % detection accuracy | not time-to-event | none |
| Askar et al. 2025, JKSU-ES | Sulfide-scale deposition (ANN) | 1,486 field incidences, chemistry | classification | none | none |
| URTeC 2025 (PHREEQC-informed ML) | Predict PHREEQC SI from TDS, pH | Permian produced water | regression | none | none |
| Lai et al. 2026, GSE 151:205911 | Water-breakthrough timing, gas wells | 9 Sichuan wells, cross-well | RMSE / MAPE | 2–6 days | none |
| **This work** | **Time to +5 % choke restriction (class 7)** | **3W 2.0, leave-one-well-out, real-only test** | best deployed 17/24 at 0.05 FA/well-day; best AUPRC@24h 0.81 | **median 2.9–4.1 h** | **ensemble + conformal (PICP 0.87)** |

† As reported for Lopes et al. 2025 in Lopes et al. 2026; the 2025 abstract was not accessible. Only numbers we could read in the source are listed; unverifiable figures from an earlier draft were removed.

**Why the numbers are not directly comparable:** (1) published 3W studies *detect* a labelled anomaly
after onset; we *forecast* a future severity level before t_E. (2) Random or sample-level splits put
windows of the same well in training and test; issue #231 shows macro-F1 falling from 0.99 to 0.30 when
whole wells are held out, which is our setting. (3) We report AUPRC, event recall and false alarms per
well-day because about 86 % of 24-h windows are negative. (4) Cheng, Askar and URTeC use field chemistry
or long well histories, not 3W. (5) We did not re-run published models; B3, B5, B7 and B9 follow their
architectures and act as proxies under our protocol.

**Where this work is new:** (i) timed (survival) forecasting of choke scaling on 3W, (ii) a
self-referenced restriction proxy (RI*) that works without the pressure across the choke, (iii) the
combination of a PHREEQC saturation index with production-sensor models, and (iv) the two 3W data issues
in §1. The unseen-well protocol itself is becoming a 3W community standard (issue #231); our evaluation
is aligned with it rather than new. The model architecture is a new combination of existing parts and
is not claimed as a contribution, especially since it does not win.

## 7. Results analysis and discussion vs gaps G1–G4

**G1 — no timed forecasting of scaling.** Addressed for the first time on 3W. The best models give a
calibrated multi-horizon risk and catch 16–17 of 24 events on unseen wells with a median lead of 3–4 h.
Horizon analysis (E5, `horizon_auprc.png`): AUPRC rises from 0.2–0.5 at 1 h to 0.7–0.8 at 24 h and
0.8–0.97 at 72 h (B7: 0.30, 0.74, 0.97). Lead time is bounded by the data: pre-onset normal data has a
median of 6.5 h and the time from window start to event a median of 12 h. Days of warning need the long
real histories 3W does not have (this is the main reason a field data set, e.g. from Mari, matters).

**G2 — chemistry and sensors never fused.** Partly addressed: (i) engineered physical indicators (RI*
trend, Joule–Thomson cooling, system pressure) are fused with learned sequence features through a gate,
and removing them costs 0.07 AUROC; (ii) a PHREEQC saturation-index prior from public brine analyses is
fused with the sensor models, with small, non-significant gains. Well-specific water chemistry is
needed for a real test.

**G3 — no unseen-well evaluation.** Fully addressed: leave-one-well-out with real-only test sets,
thresholds fixed before testing, false alarms per well-day per confounder class, and a v1 → v2
generalisation test. **Central finding:** ranking transfers across wells better than raw thresholds do.
Self-referencing the score to the instance's first hour (calib-self) roughly doubles deployed recall for
several models (e.g. B4 12 → 16 hits, B7 5 → 13) at fewer false alarms.

**G4 — rare events, private data, no uncertainty.** The data is public. Rarity is handled with a
censor-aware loss, class weighting and synthetic data in training only. Uncertainty: the deep ensemble
plus split-conformal intervals give near-nominal coverage for PI-PatchSurv (PICP 0.87 at 90 %, interval
factor about ×10 either side) and its variants, while most deep baselines under-cover (B3 0.48, B4-tuned
0.41, B8 0.38). The time-to-event error ΔT is {{DELTA_T}}.

**E3 robustness (corruptions applied to test inputs):**

| Corruption | PI-PatchSurv AUPRC@24h | LSTM (B3) AUPRC@24h |
|---|---|---|
| clean | 0.697 | 0.811 |
| Gaussian noise 1 / 3 / 5 % | 0.696 / 0.683 / 0.674 | 0.808 / 0.794 / 0.773 |
| 1 / 2 / 3 sensors dropped (value → median, mask = 1) | 0.714 / 0.725 / 0.700 (no loss) | 0.682 / 0.613 / 0.564 (−30 %) |
| one sensor frozen 60 min, undetected | 0.699 | 0.796 |
| resampled to 30 s / 1 / 2 / 5 min | 0.653 / 0.635 / 0.611 / 0.581 | 0.824 / 0.814 / 0.787 / 0.765 |

The mask-aware, channel-independent design makes PI-PatchSurv insensitive to sensor loss, the most
common failure in the field. It is more sensitive to the sampling rate than the LSTM. The resampling
test feeds the 1-minute models with data aggregated at other rates (features rebuilt per rate, models
not retrained).

**E4 generalisation (train on 3W v1 wells → test on wells new in v2, 20 events):** best ranking B8
(AUPRC@24h 0.61, AUROC 0.92), then B3 (0.48); PI-PatchSurv 0.28 (AUROC 0.84). Deployed (calib-self):
B4 9/20 at 0.05 FA/well-day; PI-PatchSurv 4/20 at 0.37. With only 4 training events (wells 1, 6, 21),
the deep sequence model without hand-made physics transfers best.

**Statistical significance:**
- Friedman over 12 fixed models, 5 folds: χ² = 19.1, p = 0.06; over 12 tuned models p = 0.06.
- {{NEMENYI_TEXT}}
- Paired instance bootstrap vs PI-PatchSurv with Holm correction: B0, B1, B9 (fixed and tuned) are
  significantly worse; no other difference survives correction.
- With 24 events, 17 from one well, the top models cannot be separated statistically. We do not claim
  a winner.

**Why do plain recurrent models do so well?** The informative signal is a slow trend in 2–3 channels.
GRU/LSTM states integrate such trends naturally; the patch Transformer splits them into 16-minute tokens
and must re-assemble them through attention with only 24 positive events to learn from. Its advantages
are robustness to missing sensors, calibrated uncertainty and the survival output, not ranking.

**Training dynamics:** calibration-well loss was lowest after the first epoch in most runs: models
over-fit the training wells within about 150 steps. Per-well normalisation or domain-adversarial training
are the most promising next steps.

**Limitations:**
1. 24 real events, 17 from one well; results are dominated by well 24.
2. RI* is a proxy, not a measured deposit; it does not control for choke-opening changes (opening
   missing in 78 %).
3. Lead time is hours, bounded by the short 3W recordings.
4. The 1-per-well-month false-alarm budget is not met; hydrates are the main confounder.
5. Ablations use one seed; the chemistry prior uses generic, not well-specific, water.
6. Model selection between survival and per-horizon heads was not done on calibration data.
7. Several publisher pages were not accessible; base-paper numbers are limited to those stated in accessible abstracts or preprints.

**Future work:** per-well domain adaptation for threshold transfer; hydrate-vs-scale discrimination
from T-JUS-CKP dynamics; a hybrid survival + per-horizon head chosen on calibration wells; field data
with long histories, cleaning logs and water analyses (e.g. a Mari pilot) for days of warning;
contributing a class-7 time-to-restriction benchmark to the 3W repository.
